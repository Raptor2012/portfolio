XR · client work

Amyloidosis in mixed reality

Visitors hold a heart in their own hands and watch it open into a cross-section, then follow plasma cells and protein fibrils to see how the disease develops. It ends in VR, inside a blood vessel.

At a busy booth, a new visitor picks up the headset every few minutes. The booth operator presses a floating restart button that recentres tracking, so the heart appears in the right place for each new person. A ghost hand shows what to grab or touch, and if nobody interacts for a minute, the story moves on by itself.

Of 19 visitors who gave feedback, 18 said they would like to try more experiences like it.

XR · client work, a university hospital

CAR-T cell therapy story in VR

A VR story for patients and doctors at a university hospital about CAR-T cell therapy for lupus and, since 2026, polymyositis and systemic sclerosis. Each disease follows seven scenes, from the cell gateway and how the disease works to the CAR-T lab, the therapy in a blood vessel and recovery in the bone marrow.

Early tests shrank the player into the body, and people disliked it. Instead, the player puts on a suit, sees it on their own body when they look down, and steps through portals into abstract versions of the body.

In 2026 the app had to move from the Meta SDK to XR Interaction Toolkit. Almost none of its interactions carried over, so instead of fixing them one by one, I rebuilt it in a fresh project. While rebuilding, I wrote the flow controller by hand, and it became the base of the content warehouse.

XR · client work

Myasthenia gravis in VR

It starts in first person, as a patient. Body tracking shows your own body, one eye is slightly blurred, a phone rings and standing up is hard. Calls from colleagues, a mirror and a diary of bad days follow, then a call from the doctor takes you into the body.

Inside, a platform carries you along a path past nerve signals, the complement system, bone marrow, the thymus and a lymph node to the disease itself. A moving ride can make people sick, so we went through several fixes after playtests: a slower ride, a vignette, speed control, a closing pod and, in the end, short trips through portals. The journey stays big while the travel stays short.

The first full playthrough lasted 30 minutes. It is now about 20, starting with an onboarding and going straight into the body.

XR · freelance client work

First aid in traffic

You drive a car in VR and witness a crash. You pull over on the safe side, set up the warning triangle and open the crashed car’s door to wake the driver. What happens next is random: the driver may be fine, may be hurt and need first aid, or may be unconscious, so you carry them out and start CPR. CPR can end with the driver waking in pain, and you put them in the recovery position, or not waking, and you call for an ambulance.

A board floating to your left shows the current step, while dialogue and highlighted objects guide you. An early version had a car hit trainees who got out on the wrong side. It made the training too hard for some people, so it was removed.

Bone Eaters

A 3D mobile game about bone health and osteoporosis. Players complete timed tasks across three levels and unlock an AR reward: a bone model, placed with plane or QR code detection, with the bone eaters animated on it. The iOS version reached the App Store on 12 November 2025.

The junior developer did most of the game work. For the web version, gyro look became mouse swipe and click turning.

XR · internal tool

Asset library

A web viewer and nightly catalogue for 3D models in the company’s repositories. A GitHub Actions workflow looks through recent changes in every repository for new FBX and GLB files, tags each model from its repository and project, and lists it in the browser and in Unity. The scan refreshes changed branches and detects duplicates without cloning repositories.
